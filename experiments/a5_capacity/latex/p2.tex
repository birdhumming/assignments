\section*{Problem 2: Output heads}

A rank-$r$ head factors the $4096\times512$ unembedding as $W_2 W_1$ with $W_1\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times512}$ (\texttt{p2\_heads.py}); the logits then live in an $r$-dimensional subspace of the 4,096 vocabulary directions. ``Full'' is the ordinary $r=512$ head. For the gradient diagnostic we take the softmax error $g=p-\mathrm{onehot}(y)$ at every position of 8 held-out sequences and report the fraction of $\|g\|^2$ outside the column space of the head, $1-\|P_{\text{col}}\,g\|^2/\|g\|^2$; a random $r$-dimensional subspace would suppress $1-r/4096$.

\textbf{(a) Rank ladder.} Loss falls steeply with head rank: 3.263 at $r=32$, 3.112 at $r=64$, 3.016 at $r=128$, 2.927 at full rank, i.e.\ the rank-32 head costs 0.34 nats (on the Problem 1 yardstick, more than the whole d4$\to$d10 range) and rank 128 still costs 0.09. The rank-128 head saves 0.4M of 34.6M parameters and about 1\% of training FLOPs, so nothing here is a good trade; the softmax bottleneck is real at $W=512$, $V=4096$.

\textbf{(b) Where the gradient goes.} The measured suppressed fraction tracks the random-subspace line to within 0.003 at every rank (0.994 vs 0.992 at $r=32$, 0.987 vs 0.984 at $r=64$, 0.972 vs 0.969 at $r=128$, 0.878 vs 0.875 at $r=512$). So the trained head's column space is \emph{not} aligned with the error gradient any better than a random subspace of the same dimension: more than 99\% of the softmax error signal is discarded before it reaches the residual stream at rank 32, and even the full head discards 7/8. The error vector $p-\mathrm{onehot}(y)$ is a dense 4,096-vector whose energy is spread over the whole vocabulary, so no 32-dimensional subspace can capture much of it; this is why the rank ladder is so steep and why the loss gap is in the \emph{learning} of the representation, not only in the expressiveness of the final logits.

\textbf{(c) SpamLang.} Each sequence is one symbol $u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(V)$ repeated 64 times, so every scored position is deterministic and the loss floor is 0; a model that has not learned to copy pays $\log V$ (dotted lines in Figure~\ref{fig:p2spam}). All runs use the one-layer four-head model, 102,400 training sequences, 2,048 validation sequences, 800 updates at batch 128 and LR $10^{-3}$.

At width 64 the model solves $V=64$ and $V=256$ essentially exactly ($3\times10^{-7}$ and $8\times10^{-6}$ nats after 800 updates), is still improving at $V=1024$ ($1.1\times10^{-3}$) and clearly struggles at $V=4096$: 0.32 nats, i.e.\ about a quarter of the sequences are still not copied, with the curve flattening. Width rescues it: at $V=4096$ the loss after 800 updates is 0.32, $5\times10^{-3}$, $2\times10^{-5}$ and $9\times10^{-7}$ for $W=64,128,256,512$, improving by two to three orders of magnitude per doubling. The copy rule \emph{is} representable at width 64 for any $V$ (any $V$ distinct unit vectors $e_u$ satisfy $\langle e_u,e_u\rangle>\langle e_u,e_v\rangle$, so a tied embedding/unembedding with a large enough logit scale copies perfectly), so the failure is a learning problem, not an expressivity one: the margin between $e_u$ and its nearest neighbour shrinks as $V/W$ grows and the logit scale needed to drive the loss down grows with it. The second, data-side reason is that draws are independent per sequence, not per repetition: 102,400 sequences give each symbol $\approx1{,}600$ training examples at $V=64$ but only $\approx25$ at $V=4096$, so the rows of the unembedding for rare symbols receive 64 times fewer gradient updates whatever the width. Counting the 64 repetitions as draws would overstate the data by that factor and make the slow $V=4096$ curve look like a capacity failure.

\textbf{(d) Beyond softmax.} Sigsoftmax changes nothing: 2.928 vs 2.927 at full rank and 3.272 vs 3.263 at rank 32. Multiplying the softmax by a sigmoid of the same logits adds a nonlinearity but no new directions, so it cannot undo a 32-dimensional bottleneck (and the gradient diagnostic above says the bottleneck is the problem). Mixture-of-softmaxes is the method that works: four rank-$r$ softmaxes with an input-dependent mixture give 3.085 at $r=32$ (vs 3.263), 2.908 at $r=128$ (vs 3.016, and better than the full-rank softmax at 2.927) and 2.920 at full rank. CPLM (a copy gate that mixes a pointer distribution over the context into the softmax) helps less but consistently: 3.140 at $r=32$ and 2.976 at $r=128$. The ordering follows what each method does to the \emph{effective rank} of the log-probability matrix. A softmax over rank-$r$ logits produces log-probabilities of rank $\le r+1$; the sigmoid factor in Sigsoftmax is a pointwise function of the same rank-$r$ logits, so it adds no new directions. A mixture of four rank-$r$ softmaxes is a log-sum-exp of four rank-$(r+1)$ matrices, which is not low-rank: at $r=32$ the four components contribute up to 132 directions before the nonlinearity and the log of the mixture is full rank in general, which is why MoS at rank 32 lands between the rank-128 and rank-256 softmax points. CPLM adds a context-dependent, non-parametric component (the pointer distribution has support on the tokens actually present in the context, a different set at every position) to the rank-$r$ logits, again breaking the low-rank structure, but it only redistributes mass onto context tokens, so its gain is bounded by how often the next token is a copy. The cost: MoS quadruples the head's output matrix multiplications and its advantage shrinks to 0.007 nats at full rank, where the bottleneck is already gone.
