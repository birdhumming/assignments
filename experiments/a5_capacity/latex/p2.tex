\section*{Problem 2. The readout bottleneck}

A rank-$r$ head factors the $4096\times512$ unembedding as $A_rB_r$ with $B_r\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times512}$ (\texttt{p2\_heads.py}), so the logits live in an $r$-dimensional subspace. ``Full'' is the ordinary rank-512 head. All runs are d8w512 at LR 0.003 unless noted. Table~\ref{tab:p2grid} gives every head; Figure~\ref{fig:p2rank} plots the rank ladder and the gradient diagnostic.

\subsection*{Part (1). Measuring the bottleneck}

\lead{The rank ladder.} Loss falls steeply with rank: 3.263 at $r=32$, 3.112 at 64, 3.016 at 128, 2.965 at 256 and 2.927 at full rank. The rank-32 head costs 0.34, more than the whole d4 to d10 range on the Problem 1 ladder, and rank 128 still costs 0.09. A rank-128 head saves 0.4M of 34.6M parameters and about 1\% of training FLOPs, so nothing here is a good trade. A higher LR does not rescue the bottlenecked head: rank 32 at LR 0.006 gives 3.271 against 3.263 at 0.003.

\lead{(i) Gradient energy.} The measured suppressed fraction $E_r$ tracks the random-subspace line to within 0.003 at every rank: 0.994 against 0.992 at $r=32$, 0.987 against 0.984 at 64, 0.972 against 0.969 at 128, 0.940 against 0.938 at 256, and 0.878 against 0.875 at 512.
\begin{itemize}
\item The trained head's column space is aligned with the error gradient no better than a random subspace of the same dimension.
\item More than 99\% of the error signal is discarded before it reaches the residual stream at rank 32. Even the full head discards 7/8.
\item The error $p-\mathrm{onehot}(y)$ is a dense 4,096-vector with energy spread over the whole vocabulary, so no 32-dimensional subspace can capture much of it. The loss gap is therefore in the learning of the representation, not only in the expressiveness of the final logits.
\end{itemize}

\lead{(ii) SpamLang.} Each sequence is one symbol $u\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(V)$ repeated 64 times. After the first symbol the next token is determined, so the loss floor is 0 and a model that has not learned to copy pays $\log V$ (dotted in Figure~\ref{fig:p2spam}). All runs use the one-layer four-head model, 102,400 training sequences, 2,048 validation sequences, 800 updates at batch 128, LR $10^{-3}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Width 64 solves small vocabularies and struggles at 4,096.} After 800 updates the loss is $3\times10^{-7}$ at $V=64$, $8\times10^{-6}$ at 256, $1.1\times10^{-3}$ at 1,024 (still improving) and 0.32 at 4,096, where about a quarter of the sequences are still not copied and the curve is flattening.
\item \lead{Width rescues it.} At $V=4096$ the loss is 0.32, $5\times10^{-3}$, $2\times10^{-5}$ and $9\times10^{-7}$ for $W=64,128,256,512$: two to three orders of magnitude per doubling.
\item \lead{This is a learning problem, not expressivity.} Any $V$ distinct unit vectors satisfy $\langle e_u,e_u\rangle>\langle e_u,e_v\rangle$, so a tied head with a large enough logit scale copies perfectly at width 64 for any $V$. But the margin to the nearest neighbour shrinks as $V/W$ grows, and the logit scale needed grows with it.
\item \lead{Count draws, not repetitions.} Draws are independent per sequence, so 102,400 sequences give each symbol about 1,600 examples at $V=64$ but only 25 at $V=4096$. The rows of the unembedding for rare symbols get 64 times fewer updates whatever the width. Counting the 64 repetitions as draws would make the slow $V=4096$ curve look like a capacity failure.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Part (2). Addressing the bottleneck}

\begin{itemize}
\item \lead{Sigsoftmax changes nothing.} 2.928 against 2.927 at full rank, 3.272 against 3.263 at rank 32, 3.019 against 3.016 at rank 128. Multiplying the softmax by a sigmoid of the same logits adds a nonlinearity but no new directions, so it cannot undo a 32-dimensional bottleneck, and the gradient diagnostic says the bottleneck is the problem.
\item \lead{Mixture of softmaxes works.} Four rank-$r$ softmaxes with an input-dependent mixture give 3.085 at $r=32$ (against 3.263), %MOS64% 2.908 at 128 (against 3.016, and better than the full-rank softmax), 2.931 at 256 (against 2.965) and 2.920 at full rank. MoS at rank 32 lands between the rank-128 and rank-256 softmax points, and MoS at rank 128 beats the rank-256 softmax, so the mixture is worth roughly a factor of 4 in rank. The non-monotone MoS curve (rank 128 better than rank 256) is within two seed deviations of flat; the mixture gains nothing once $r\ge128$.
\item \lead{CPLM helps less but consistently.} A copy gate that mixes a pointer distribution over the context into the softmax gives 3.140 at $r=32$, %CPLM64% 2.976 at 128, 2.937 at 256 and 2.919$^\dagger$ at full rank (the dense softmax scores 2.935 on the same diagnostic set, so CPLM is about 0.015 better once the bottleneck is gone). Its gain is bounded by how often the next token is a copy of the context.
\item \lead{Why the ordering.} A softmax over rank-$r$ logits gives log-probabilities of rank at most $r+1$. The sigmoid factor in Sigsoftmax is a pointwise function of the same logits, so it adds no new directions. A mixture of four rank-$r$ softmaxes is a log-sum-exp of four rank-$(r+1)$ matrices, which is full rank in general. CPLM adds a non-parametric component whose support is the set of tokens present in the context, a different set at every position, which also breaks the low-rank structure but only moves mass onto context tokens.
\item \lead{Cost.} MoS quadruples the head's matrix multiplications, and its advantage shrinks to 0.007 at full rank, where the bottleneck is already gone.
\end{itemize}

\lead{Synthesis.} The softmax bottleneck is real at $W=512$, $V=4096$: the loss rises by 0.09 at rank 128 and 0.34 at rank 32, and the trained head passes back no more of the error gradient than a random subspace would. SpamLang shows that even a representable copy rule is slow to learn when $V/W$ is large and each symbol is rare. Of the three fixes, MoS is the only one that recovers rank-128 loss from a rank-32 head; Sigsoftmax does nothing; CPLM is a small, rank-independent gain.

\lead{Rules of thumb.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Softmax head: $\Delta L = +0.34, +0.19, +0.09, +0.04$ at rank 32, 64, 128, 256 from the full head (2.927).
\item Suppressed gradient energy $E_r \approx 1 - r/4096$ to within 0.003; even the full head suppresses 0.88.
\item At rank 32: MoS $-0.18$, CPLM $-0.12$, Sigsoftmax $+0.01$ relative to softmax. At rank 256: MoS $-0.034$, CPLM $-0.028$. At full rank: MoS $-0.007$, CPLM $-0.015$, Sigsoftmax $+0.001$.
\item SpamLang at width 64: solved to $10^{-3}$ or better up to $V=1024$; 0.32 at $V=4096$ after 800 updates; each doubling of width at $V=4096$ buys two to three orders of magnitude.
\end{itemize}
