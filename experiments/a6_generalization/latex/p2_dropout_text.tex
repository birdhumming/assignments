Dropout on the attention probabilities is the standard remedy for the memorization seen with Hyperball and WD .1, and the natural partner for ensembling (Part (iii)). Table~\ref{tab:drop} gives the rates we ran at the baseline recipe.
\begin{itemize}
\item A light rate helps: dropout .05 ends at 1.2591, 0.0036 below the no-dropout baseline (1.2627), which is as large as the gain from a second ensemble member on the supplied bank (0.0048) and larger than anything in the supplied parameter sweep from d6 upward.
\item The effect is non-monotone. Rate .15 (1.2623) is back at the baseline and .25 (1.2633) slightly above it: the extra noise slows fitting all the way through (1.284, 1.286 and 1.289 at pass 16 for .05, .15 and .25), and only the lightest rate recovers that cost during the LR decay.
\item Seed noise is about 0.001: our paired seed-43 baseline gives 1.2618 against the supplied 1.2627, and dropout .15 at seeds 43 and 44 gives 1.2600 and 1.2602, so across three seeds dropout .15 is worth 0.002 and dropout .05 (one seed) 0.004.
\item So with WD 3.2 already stopping memorization, dropout is a small second-order regularizer with a narrow sweet spot near .05, not a large independent lever.
\end{itemize}
